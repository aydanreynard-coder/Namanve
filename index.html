    How the shift works

    This is one planned 12-hour day shift, 06:00–18:00. Every lorry, machine, load and person follows a timetable worked out when the page opens, so you can play it at any speed, pause it or drag to any moment and see exactly what is happening there. It is an illustration built on the site model, not a record of a real day. The volumes, rates and staffing below are assumptions.

    Lorries

    • 40 lorries come in off the access road. Security checks each one at the entrance gate: one guard takes the papers at the cab while a second walks round to the rear. Then the gate swings open.
    • Each lorry is weighed in (the board shows plate and weight), then stops at marshal A's line in the west lane until he waves it on to its dock or the yard. Lorries pull past their dock, open their doors and reverse on.
    • Leaving, a lorry pulls off the dock and marshal B waves it into the east lane. It shuts its doors, weighs out, has a second security check at the exit gate and turns onto the road.
    • Nothing collides. The site is split into zones (gate, weighbridges, west lane, apron, each dock, east lane, yard bay), and a lorry books every zone it will touch before it moves, the way a dock-appointment system works. When a zone ahead is taken, it waits at a safe place: the gate, marshal A's line, its dock or the weighbridge.

    Docks and the warehouse

    • Docks 1–2, coffee in: 360 × 60 kg jute bags per lorry. A crew of four porters carries them by hand into a receiving stack behind the dock (about 45 minutes per lorry).
    • Bühler plant: runs at 6 t/h all shift. Two porters feed its intake grate from receiving stack B, and an elevator lifts the coffee to the cleaning silo.
    • Docks 3–4, bulk export: an empty 20 ft container with a bulk liner reverses onto the dock in front of the plant. The operator connects the blower hose, and the bulk blower fills it with 21 t of processed coffee at 24 t/h (about 53 minutes). He then takes the hose off and the lorry weighs out full.
    • Docks 5–6, FMCG: 22 pallets per lorry. A forklift at each dock moves them between the trailer and a floor lane of 22 slots. A reach truck per lane puts deliveries away into the racking afterwards and brings dispatch orders down to the lane before their lorry arrives.
    • Dock 7 has a lorry standing on it all shift, as drawn.

    Container yard

    • Container lorries stop in the bay beside the container rows. The reach stacker lifts their 20 ft box off and stacks it at the east end of the rows either side of aisle 1, or picks a box and sets it on the trailer. It carries boxes above the stacks and never crosses a lorry lane.

    Daylight

    • The sun follows the clock for Kampala in late September: dark with site lights on at 06:00, sunrise about 06:38, sun high around 12:40, low in the west by 18:00. Turn off "Daylight follows the clock" to keep the midday light.

    Bühler processing plant

    • Plan from Bühler drawing 0301328276-CIV-0001-02 (the ±0.00 m and −2.50 m plan view). I read it straight from the DWG, so footprints, columns, platforms, stairs, machines, pits, the compressor room and the control panel room are all as drawn, at 1:1.
    • The plan is registered to this model on the dividing wall and the south wall, then shifted 2.388 m west, so its nearest part (the compressor room) is 3.00 m from the dividing wall. The orange floor marker shows that gap. Bühler's drawing had it 0.61 m off the wall.
    • The DWG has no heights, so levels and the main equipment are matched to Bühler's 3D views of the plant: working levels about 4.0 m and 7.0 m, frames to about 10.2 m, a hopper-bottomed bin in each structure, the six-legged cleaning silo with twin hoppers, four roof exhaust units, and 4 m rooms. The exhaust ducts run east under the roof and come up through the strip beside the valley that the solar layout leaves clear, so they miss the modules. These are read off pictures, so allow about ±0.5 m. Bühler's elevation drawings would make them exact. Colours are realistic finishes, not the CAD colours in the renders.
    • Bühler's drawing shows this shed at 57.5 × 100.26 m against the site layout's 57.2 × 100.0 m. The plant follows the dividing wall.
